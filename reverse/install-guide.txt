WHAT REA-VIDEO IS
-----------------
rea-video is a free, open-source skill by JuneYaooo that reverse-engineers
any video, shot by shot. Give it a video you admire and it tells you exactly
how it was made — so you can remake the method with your own material,
instead of guessing from watching it.

It runs as a skill inside Claude Code and Codex.

THE HONEST LIMIT — READ THIS FIRST
----------------------------------
rea-video does NOT render the finished remake for you. It gives you the
plan — the recipe. The remake itself is a separate step, done by you (or
by your editor, or by Claude with an editing skill). If anyone tells you
"it remakes the video for you", that is not what this tool does.

What it does give you is unusually complete: a shot-by-shot breakdown, a
remake plan, and a readable report. That is the whole point — you stop
copying a look by eye and start rebuilding it from measured facts.

WHAT YOU GET FROM ONE BREAKDOWN
--------------------------------
For each shot, rea-video labels how it was made:

- filmed — shot on camera
- AI-generated — made by a generative video model
- coded — rendered from code / a template (for example Remotion or HTML)
- 3D — a 3D render
- screen recording — captured from a screen
- avatar — an avatar / talking-head render

And for the whole video it maps:

- Cut positions — where every shot starts and ends, and how long each one lasts
- Beats — the rhythm of the edit: cuts per minute, average shot length, transitions
- Human vs AI voice — whether the voiceover reads as a human recording or a TTS voice (this is an inference — see "Trust, but check" below)
- Music BPM — the tempo of the music bed, plus its key and where sections change
- Styled text and captions — every subtitle line, its timing, colour, position, and entrance animation

The output is a shot-by-shot breakdown, a remake plan, and a report.html
(in VIDEO.md format) you can open in a browser.

REQUIREMENTS
------------
Before installing, you need two things on your machine:

1. ffmpeg — for reading and cutting video.
2. Python 3.10 or newer.

And you need Claude Code or Codex, because rea-video runs as a skill
inside one of them — it is not a standalone app you double-click.

Do not confuse rea-video with "REA — Reverse Engineer Anything". That is
a different tool (morluto/rea): it reverse-engineers software binaries
through MCP. rea-video reverse-engineers videos. Same first three
letters, completely different job.

-------
Option A — the one-liner (easiest):

That installs the skill. Then open Claude Code or Codex and prompt it

RUN YOUR FIRST BREAKDOWN
------------------------
Pick a short video you want to learn from — a reel, a TikTok, a Short,
ideally 15–60 seconds. Save it somewhere you can point to, then prompt:

    break down this video: /path/to/video.mp4

Replace /path/to/video.mp4 with the real path to your file. That is the
whole command — there is nothing else to configure for a first run.

Give it a few minutes. A 30-second reel takes a few minutes to analyse;
longer videos take longer. Do not interrupt it mid-run — the report is
written at the end.

HOW TO READ THE REPORT
----------------------
When the run finishes you get a folder of output. The two files to open
first are:

- VIDEO.md — the master breakdown. Start here. It has the verdict (how
  the video was made, overall), the narrative line (what each stretch of
  the video is doing), and a map of every other file.
- report.html — the same report as a page you can open in any browser
  and scroll through. Easier to read than the Markdown if you just want
  the story.
- report.md — the same report in Markdown, if you prefer text.

The shot list
~~~~~~~~~~~~~
The heart of the report. One row per shot, with: start and end time,
length, what the shot says or does, what is on screen, the camera move,
the overlays, how it was made (inferred), and the remake route for that
shot.

Read it in order and ask one question per shot: what job is this shot
doing? Hook, proof, reveal, payoff — the job matters more than the effect.

The remake plan (video/remake.md)
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
This is the practical part. It tells you, in order: plan the content from
the narrative line, collect the real material you need (photos, screen
recordings), rebuild the motion-graphics shots in code, edit to the
measured cut lengths, then add the sound. It also lists exactly which real
files you must supply yourself — rea-video cannot invent your screenshots
or your footage.

Shot labels — filmed, AI, coded, 3D, screen recording, avatar
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
Each shot carries a label and a confidence percentage. A label of "code /
template render 91%" means the tool is fairly sure that shot was rendered
from code. Treat the label as a strong hint, not a fact carved in stone —
and check it against the frames, which are saved alongside the report.

Music, voice, and sound effects
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
- Music BPM — the tempo, so you can find or make a track at the same
  speed. The report also notes the key and where the music changes section.
- Voice — whether the voiceover looks human or TTS, and how much of the
  video is voice.
- Sound effects — every effect hit, with its time and what it lands on
  (a subtitle popping in, a cut, a sticker). The separated audio clips are
  saved in sfx/, so you can listen to each one before finding a matching
  effect in your own library.
- Stems — the separated stems/ folder holds the voice, music, and effects
  tracks apart (vocals.wav, music.wav, effects.wav), plus the original.
  Listening to the effects stem on its own is the fastest way to hear what
  the sound design is actually doing.

Captions and styled text
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
The packaging/ folder holds the subtitle files (subtitles.srt,
overlays.ass, timeline.json). The report lists every subtitle line with
its time, style, and entrance — hard cut in, scale pop, type-on, and so
on. If you are remaking the video, these files are your caption timing:
burn them in with ffmpeg (-vf ass=overlays.ass) or rebuild them from
timeline.json in your editor.

Frames and scenes
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
- shots/ — first frame, last frame, a key-frame strip, and crops of every
  element for each shot. Use these to copy layout, type size, and margins
  by eye.
- frames/ — full-size frames per scene, plus the drawn layer cut out on
  its own, so you can trace shapes or rebuild a scene instead of guessing
  from a description.
- storyboard.jpg — the whole video on one page. Glance at this first if
  you want the shape of the video before reading anything.
- breakdown.json — the full raw data, for programs. You will probably
  never need it.

WHAT A REAL REPORT LOOKS LIKE — FROM A RUN ON OUR MACHINE
----------------------------------------------------------
We ran rea-video on a real 32.7-second reel on 13 November 2026. Here is
what came back, so you know what to expect from your own first run:

- 8 shots, 4.1 seconds each on average
- Made with: code / template render, 100% of the duration
- Confidence: 82%
- Music: 136 BPM, F major, running for 61% of the video
- Sound effects: 16 hits, 14 distinct sounds (mostly whooshes, plus clicks)
- Subtitles: 43 subtitle lines, with entrances split between hard cuts in, scale pops, and type-on
- Edit rhythm: 12.9 cuts per minute; transitions were a mix of hard cuts, wipes, a dissolve, a blur, and a zoom
- Real material needed for the remake: a photo (in 2 shots) and a screen recording (in 1 shot) — the rest was code-rendered

Your numbers will be different. The shape of the report will be the same.

TRUST, BUT CHECK
----------------
rea-video labels things as seen (visible on screen, or measured directly)
or inferred (the tool's judgement, with its basis listed). When the two
disagree, trust the frames.

Every report also has an Open questions section — things the tool could
not settle, such as whether a voice is really TTS or human, or whether
the music is a stock-library track. In our real run, the music source and
the voice source were both listed as "to confirm", with the tool's best
guess and how to check it (for music: Shazam it, or check the music tag
on the original post). Treat those flags as homework, not as answers.

REMAKE IT YOURSELF — CHECKLIST
------------------------------
Use this after your first breakdown. Work in this order:

1. Write the narrative line first. One line per stretch: hook, evidence,
   reveal, payoff. Keep the same order and the same job at each step, but
   refill it with your own cases, numbers, and material. Copying the
   structure is learning; copying the pictures is not.
2. List the real material you need. Photos, screen recordings, footage —
   the report tells you which shots need real files. Collect those before
   you build anything.
3. Rebuild the coded / motion-graphics shots. If a shot is labelled code /
   template render, rebuild it in Remotion or HTML from the text tracks
   and layout in the report. The frames and element crops are your
   reference.
4. Cut to the measured lengths. Use the shot list's exact times. Same
   order, same lengths, same transitions where you can match them.
5. Add captions from the packaging files. Same words, same timing, same
   entrances — with your own text.
6. Add sound last. Music at the measured BPM, then one effect per hit at
   the listed times. Listen to the sfx/ clips first and match each one.
7. Compare side by side. Watch yours next to the original: shot lengths,
   subtitle positions, where the music comes in and drops. Fix the
   worst-matching shots first.
