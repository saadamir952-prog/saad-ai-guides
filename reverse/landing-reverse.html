The full guide

What rea-video is. A free, open-source skill by JuneYaooo (Apache-2.0, created 29 October 2026, v0.1.0 released 11 November 2026) that runs inside Claude Code and Codex. 11,440 stars as of 21 November 2026 — up 2,800 in the previous 7 days, crossed 10,000 stars on 17 November. Repo: github.com/JuneYaooo/rea-video.

Do not confuse it with “REA — Reverse Engineer Anything” (morluto/rea) — that tool reverse-engineers software binaries. rea-video reverse-engineers videos.

What you get. Every shot labelled filmed, AI-generated, coded, 3D, screen recording, or avatar — plus cut positions, beats, human vs AI voice, music BPM, and styled text/captions. Output: a shot-by-shot breakdown, a remake plan, and a report.html (VIDEO.md format).

Requirements. ffmpeg, Python 3.10+, and Claude Code or Codex.

Install. npx skills add JuneYaooo/rea-video — or install from the GitHub repo and follow its README.

First breakdown. Prompt: “break down this video: /path/to/video.mp4”. A 30-second reel takes a few minutes; do not interrupt it — the report is written at the end.

How to read the report. Start with VIDEO.md (verdict, narrative line, map of every file), then report.html in a browser. The shot list is the heart: one row per shot with its time, job, camera move, and remake route. video/remake.md is the practical plan — content, real material to collect, code rebuild, edit, sound. packaging/ holds the subtitle files (subtitles.srt, overlays.ass, timeline.json). sfx/ holds a clip of each sound effect; stems/ holds the voice, music, and effects tracks apart. shots/ and frames/ hold the frames and element crops to rebuild from. storyboard.jpg shows the whole video on one page.

What a real report looks like. Our run on a 32.7-second reel, 13 November 2026: 8 shots (4.1s average), code / template render 100%, confidence 82%, music 136 BPM in F major, 16 sound-effect hits (14 distinct), 43 subtitle lines, 12.9 cuts per minute. Real material needed: a photo and a screen recording — the rest was code-rendered.

Trust, but check. Things are labelled seen (measured) or inferred (judgement). When they disagree, trust the frames. The Open questions section lists what the tool could not settle — in our run, the music and voice sources were both “to confirm”. Treat those as homework, not answers.

Remake it yourself. (1) Write the narrative line first — same order and job per stretch, your own cases and numbers. (2) List the real material you need. (3) Rebuild the coded shots in Remotion or HTML. (4) Cut to the measured lengths. (5) Add captions from the packaging files. (6) Add sound last — music at the measured BPM, one effect per hit. (7) Compare side by side and fix the worst-matching shots first.
