The method in 30 seconds

1
Drop the clip in the folder. One folder, your raw clips inside. No bins, no timeline, no prep — the folder IS the project.
2
Pick your takes. Watch the clips once, keep the ones you want. This is the one step Claude doesn’t do — the choice is yours.
3
Type one line. Edit my video: cut silences, add captions, export vertical. That is the whole brief. Claude reads it and runs the three jobs.
4
Walk away. The run takes seconds, not hours. Come back, watch the export, fix anything you don’t like with one more line.

The 3 jobs (real measured times)

The run in the video was timed live, on a real edit, with a stopwatch on screen. The exact measured times:

  • Cut the silences — 7.2 s. Every dead gap in the voiceover is detected and cut. No more scrubbing through a timeline hunting for pauses.
  • Generate the captions — 5.2 s. Word-by-word captions, timed to the speech, burned into the export.
  • Master the sound — 19.5 s. The audio chain (EQ, compression, loudness) plus the final export render. This is the slowest job because it includes the encode.
  • Total: 32.1 seconds (shown as 00:32 in the video). The same edit by hand took three hours.

Your times will vary with clip length and machine speed — longer clips take longer to export. But the shape holds: the mechanical jobs take seconds, and the hours disappear.

Honest limits

  • You still pick the takes. Claude cuts the silences and adds the captions — it does not decide which shot is the good one. Watch your clips once, keep the ones you want, drop the rest. The taste is yours.
  • No colour grading. The skill does not grade colour. If your footage needs a look, grade it yourself first (or accept the footage as shot). The edit will be clean; the colour will be whatever your camera gave you.
  • You need Claude Code. The editing happens inside Claude Code, which needs a paid Claude plan. This is not a free web tool you open in a tab.
  • The first draft is rarely the keeper. Budget for one review pass: watch the export, tell Claude what to fix in one more line, re-export. The loop is fast because the run is seconds.
  • Export time scales with length. A 30-second reel exports in seconds; a 10-minute video takes minutes. The 32-second number is for a short vertical reel — don’t expect it on a feature film.
