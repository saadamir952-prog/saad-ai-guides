The exact prompt (copy-paste)

Put three of your best reels and one raw clip in one folder, open Claude Code there, and paste this:

Study these three reels — captions, cuts, pacing, hooks — write my style sheet, then edit this clip to match it.

Then read what it writes back. The style sheet is the deliverable: a short list of your rules, in plain words. If a line is wrong, correct it — the sheet is yours to edit, and every correction makes the next edit closer.

The style-sheet template

This is the shape Claude writes back. Use it to check the sheet, or fill it in yourself first and let Claude refine it:

MY STYLE SHEET — [your handle]

1. VOICEOVER PACE: [words per second, measured from my reels]
2. CAPTIONS: [word-by-word / per sentence · words per phrase]
3. FRAME 1: [what fills the first frame — the result, the number]
4. CUTS: [morphs / hard cuts / what carries across]
5. PACE: [something new every ~X seconds]
6. PROOF: [the result is shown before it is explained]
7. CTA: [keyword at ~X% of the video]
8. SOUND: [music / no music · sound on every text appearance]
9. PRESENTER: [face or avatar, where, how big]
10. ACCENT: [one accent colour per video]

My filled example (measured from @saad.aig)

  1. Voiceover pace: 3.0–3.7 words/sec
  2. Captions: word-by-word, 2–4 words per phrase
  3. Frame 1: one huge thing (the finished result, first second)
  4. Cuts: morphs — handoffs carry a shared element
  5. Pace: something new roughly every second
  6. Proof: the result on screen before the explanation
  7. CTA: keyword at ~85% of the video
  8. Sound: no music; a sound on every text appearance
  9. Presenter: small avatar, hook and lead only
  10. Accent: one accent colour per video

The 3-step checklist

1

One folder. Three of your best reels — your best, not your latest — plus the raw clip you want edited.

2

One prompt. Paste the prompt above. Read the style sheet it writes back. Correct any line that is not you.

3

Next time, one line. No setup. “Edit this like my reels.” It already knows how — the sheet is saved in the folder.

The catch

The sheet is only as good as the reels you feed it. Generic reels in, generic sheet out — Claude studies what you show it, and it cannot invent a style you do not have. Feed it your three most YOU videos, the ones people save and share, not the ones that merely did numbers. And read the sheet before you trust the edit: if Claude writes a rule you never follow, strike it. The sheet is a draft of your taste, and you are still the editor.
