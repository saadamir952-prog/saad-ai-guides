# Edit Before You Film — The Mock-Up Hack

**Free guide · Keyword: MOCKUP · Saad AI**

## The cost — read this first

This method is **free**. The mock-up uses a **free local voice** (Kokoro,
free and open-source, runs on your own machine) and rough visuals you draw
yourself — grey cards, plain text, no grade, no polish. Claude Code needs a
paid Claude plan — that is the one paid part, and you already need it for
the script step.

**Do not confuse this with the paid version.** Riley Brown's reel uses
ElevenLabs voice cloning (paid) for his mock-up voice. This guide teaches
the **free version**: a stand-in voice that sounds like a stand-in. It is
supposed to sound rough. The mock-up is a draft, not the video.

## The idea in one sentence

Pixar edits the movie before they make it — you can do the same with your
phone: build a watchable rough version first, fix the weak line in the
mock-up, then film once.

## The proof

Riley Brown (23 Nov 2026 reel) films nothing first. He writes the script,
Claude builds a mock-up — a 3D avatar, a cloned voice, lip-sync, styled
from his past videos — he watches it, prints the script, films the real
take, and Claude swaps the real performance in. **He says 90% stays the
same.** That is his claim about his own workflow, not our measured number.

Our own demo, in the video: the same line twice. The rough mock-up —
grey card, monospace, DRAFT stamp, stand-in voice — and the filmed take,
the polished version of the same line. The hook changed in the mock-up,
not on set.

## The exact prompt (copy-paste)

Paste this into Claude Code, then fill in the brackets:

```
You are my video editor. Before I film anything, build me a watchable
mock-up of this video.

Topic: [YOUR TOPIC, one sentence]
Audience: [WHO IT IS FOR]
One idea: [THE ONE THING THE VIEWER WALKS AWAY WITH]

1. Write the script first — Hook (0–3 s), Lead, Body (3 steps max),
   CTA. Keep it under 130 words, about 30 seconds spoken.
2. Make the mock-up: read the script aloud in a stand-in voice (any
   free local voice — I use Kokoro, free), and lay it over rough
   visuals — grey cards, plain text, no grade, no polish. Time every
   visual to the script.
3. I watch the mock-up like a viewer. Tell me: which line is weak?
   Which second loses me? Rewrite the weak line in the mock-up,
   not on camera.

Rules: the mock-up is a draft, not the video. A stand-in voice is not
my delivery. I film once, after the mock-up is fixed.
```

## The 3-step checklist

1. **Script in Claude.** Hook, lead, body, CTA. Under 130 words. One
   idea, one technique, one video.
2. **Mock-up, timed to the script.** Rough voice + rough visuals.
   If it looks finished, it is not a mock-up — make it rougher.
3. **Watch it like a viewer.** The weak line gets rewritten BEFORE
   the camera rolls. If nothing changes between the mock-up and the
   film, you watched it like an editor, not a viewer — watch it again.

## The catch

A mock-up is a draft, not the video. It cannot replace the real take,
and a cloned voice is not your delivery. If the mock-up sounds good
enough to ship, you have not made a mock-up — you have made a bad
video. The mock-up's job is to be wrong cheaply: mistakes are free in
the mock-up. On camera, they cost you five takes.
