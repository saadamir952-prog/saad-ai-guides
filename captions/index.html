The full guide

The rule in one line. Captions are not subtitles. Subtitles transcribe; captions sell the watch. One word at a time, each word on the frame it was spoken, one keyword to land on.

Why it works. A viewer decides in the first second whether to keep watching, and the captions are the first thing their eye tracks. A full sentence on screen is a wall — nobody reads a wall, they scroll past it. One word at a time gives the eye a rhythm to follow, and a highlighted keyword gives it somewhere to land. The timing is the part people miss: a caption that arrives after the word was spoken feels broken — the eye and the ear disagree, and the viewer leaves without knowing why. A caption that lands on the exact frame the word is spoken feels invisible, and invisible is the point.

The caption checklist. (1) One word at a time — never the whole sentence at once. (2) Every word on the frame it was spoken — not early, not late. (3) One word to land on — highlight the keyword in each line. (4) Big enough to read at a glance — test it on a phone, at arm’s length. (5) Keyword pops, not decoration — a pop draws the eye to the word that matters; popping every word is the same as popping none.

Mistake one — the whole sentence at once. The full sentence appears the moment the speaker starts it and sits there until the sentence ends. The viewer reads ahead, gets bored, and scrolls — or doesn’t read at all, because a wall of text looks like work. Fix: show one word at a time, each word appearing on the frame it is spoken.

Mistake two — every word looks the same. Same size, same weight, same color, every word. Nothing tells the eye where the meaning is, so the eye wanders — and a wandering eye scrolls. Fix: pick the keyword in each line and make it the one thing that stands out. One highlighted word per line is enough. Two is a competition; three is noise.

The catch. Captions can’t save a boring video — they sell a good one. If the video itself has nothing worth watching, perfect captions just make the boredom legible. Make the video worth selling first; the captions are how you sell it.
