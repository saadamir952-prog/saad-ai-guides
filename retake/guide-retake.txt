RETAKE: Film Messy — Claude Keeps the Last Take — Free Guide
By Saad AI — free guide. Keyword: RETAKE.

THE RULE IN ONE LINE
--------------------
Shoot one continuous take. Never stop the recording. When you flub a
line, restart the whole sentence — never pick up mid-sentence. Claude
edits from the transcript and keeps only the last version of each line.

WHY IT WORKS
------------
Claude never watches your video. It reads the transcript. When the same
sentence appears three times in a row, the transcript makes the pattern
obvious: attempt one, attempt two, attempt three — and only the last one
is the keeper. Claude cuts the earlier versions and the pauses between
them, and the take that is left sounds like you nailed it first time.

That only works if the transcript can tell the attempts apart. That is
why the filming rule matters, and why the mid-sentence catch matters most.

THE FILMING CHECKLIST
---------------------
Do this before you press record. The whole method depends on it.

1. One continuous take. Press record once. Do not stop until the whole
   section is done. Stopping and starting again creates separate clips,
   and Claude cannot see the attempts as one sequence.
2. Restart the sentence, never the recording. Flub a word? Pause, take
   a breath, and say the whole sentence again from the start.
3. Never pick up mid-sentence. Do not restart from the word you messed
   up. A half-sentence in the transcript looks like a new line, not a
   repeat — Claude cannot tell which half is the keeper.
4. Leave a beat between attempts. A short pause after a flub is what
   separates the attempts in the transcript. Do not rush straight into
   the retry.
5. Say the sentence the same way each time. Same words, same order. If
   you change the wording, it is not a repeat — it is a different line,
   and Claude will keep both.
6. Keep going after the good one. One clean take is enough. Move on to
   the next sentence; do not re-record a line that already landed.

THE MID-SENTENCE CATCH — THE ONE MISTAKE THAT BREAKS IT
--------------------------------------------------------
This is the mistake that kills the method: you flub the last word of a
sentence and restart from that word.

Say the sentence is "Claude keeps the last take." You say "Claude keeps
the last—" and stop. Then you say "take." and move on. In the transcript
that reads as two fragments, not two attempts. Claude has no clean version
to keep and no repeat to cut — it either keeps both fragments, or cuts
the wrong piece.

Restart the whole sentence instead: "Claude keeps the last—" … pause …
"Claude keeps the last take." Now the transcript shows a false start and
a full clean version. Claude keeps the clean one and cuts the false
start. That is the entire trick.

If you remember one thing: restart the sentence, never the word.

DIRECT THE EDIT IN ONE-SENTENCE COMMANDS
----------------------------------------
Once the take is filmed, tell Claude what you want in plain English —
one sentence per job. These three cover almost every messy take:

- "Cut the silences."
- "Punch in on the hook."
- "Subtitles on."

Say them one at a time, check the result, then give the next one. Short
commands keep the edit predictable: each one does one job, and if
something looks wrong you know which command caused it.

THE EXACT PROMPT — PASTE THIS INTO CLAUDE CODE
----------------------------------------------
Copy this, replace the file name with your own, and paste it into
Claude Code:

    Transcribe messy-take.mp4. For every sentence I repeated, keep only
    the last version of it and cut the earlier attempts. Cut the pauses
    between attempts. Then punch in on the hook and add word-by-word
    subtitles.

That is the whole prompt. It says the four things Claude needs to do,
in order:

1. Transcribe — read the take as text.
2. Keep only the last version of each repeated sentence — the retake
   rule, applied to the transcript.
3. Cut the pauses — remove the hesitations between attempts.
4. Punch in on the hook and add word-by-word subtitles — the finish.

If the result keeps a repeat you wanted gone, point at it in plain
words: "Cut the first two attempts of the sentence about the last take."
Claude re-cuts from the transcript — you never touch a timeline.

HONEST LIMITS
-------------
- Claude Code is paid. The filming rule is free, the measuring tools are
  free, the seat is not.
- It only fixes repeats you filmed as repeats. If you picked up
  mid-sentence, the transcript has fragments, not attempts — re-film the
  line properly instead of asking Claude to guess.
- It keeps the last version, not the best version. If your last attempt
  is the worst one, say the sentence once more. The keeper is always the
  final attempt.
- Check the export before you post. Watch the finished take once, end
  to end. If a cut lands mid-word, tell Claude which sentence and it
  re-cuts.
- The numbers above are from one take on our machine. Your messy take
  will have different numbers. The shape of the result — raw take in,
  clean last attempt out — is the same.
