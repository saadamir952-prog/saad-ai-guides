The setup, step by step

1. Make the project folder. One folder per video: raw clip, audio, graphics, export. The whole run stays repeatable.

2. Transcribe the raw take. Whisper gives word timings; mark retakes, blinks-and-restarts, and dead air. Check names and numbers first — timings are close, not perfect.

3. Cut first, decorate later. Cut retakes and pauses with ffmpeg from the transcript marks, short crossfades on the joins. The tightened take is the spine.

4. Transcribe the tightened take to words.json. One row per word: word, start, end. These timings — never hand-typed timestamps — drive every graphic.

5. Write the shot plan. One row per shot: {layout, anchor_phrase, content}. The anchor phrase is matched against words.json, so the shot starts when those words are said:

{layout: "finished-frame", anchor_phrase: "four bedrooms", content: "big 4 fills on the word Four"}
{layout: "chart-behind", anchor_phrase: "a chart rises", content: "bars rise behind the speaker — only spoken numbers"}
{layout: "measure", anchor_phrase: "twelve-meter pool", content: "measure line draws across the pool"}
{layout: "shot-plan", anchor_phrase: "shot plan", content: "rows appear, matched to the voice"}

Only put numbers in the chart that the speaker actually says. No invented stats, no fake prices.

6. Render from the plan, by code. The render looks up each anchor phrase in words.json and places the graphic on that word. Change a row, re-render, done — nobody opened a timeline.

7. Captions last, on the final audio. Text from your script, timing from Whisper on the finished audio. Highlight the current word; keep chunks readable.

8. Check before you post. Watch once with sound, once muted: no silent gaps, every number on its word, chart numbers all spoken, measure graphic on the pool line.

The honest catch

This will not save a boring take. The cuts and graphics are timed to your words — your words still have to land.
